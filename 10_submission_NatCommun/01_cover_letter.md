# Cover letter

**To the Editors, *Nature Communications***

---

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, **"Thermoregulation sets how strongly climate
filters vertebrate assemblages, and reserves leave a directional functional gap"**, for
publication as an Article.

**What the paper reports.** Trait-based biogeography has a standard move: collapse many
traits into one composite axis, then ask which environmental gradient explains it. Using
2,837 terrestrial vertebrate species across 3,782 50-km cells of China, we show that both
steps discard information, and that what is discarded is exactly what conservation planning
needs.

Three results carry the paper.

1. **Assemblage trait structure is at least three separable dimensions, and compositing
   them costs sensitivity.** Body size, pace of life and niche breadth are near-independent
   (|*r*| ≤ 0.30), and combining traits into a composite axis *weakens* the environmental
   signal in six of eight comparisons. The conventional "PCA-then-regress" pipeline therefore
   understates trait–environment coupling.

2. **Climate dominates in every class, but thermoregulatory mode sets how strongly.** Ambient
   energy affects ectotherm assemblages about 2.3 times more strongly than endotherm
   assemblages, stable across specifications (1.98–2.63; every 95% CI excluding 1). We test
   this with the fourth-corner max test rather than naive community-weighted-mean
   regression, having first quantified the latter's Type I inflation on these data
   (1.27–1.72-fold, worst in ectotherms).

3. **The reserve network's functional shortfall has a direction, and the direction reverses
   between classes.** The network covers 10.36% of China's land but brings only 2.9% of
   species to the Kunming–Montreal target of 30% of range protected; an optimal layout of the
   same area would reach 50.7%. Large mammals are the best-protected trait end (+0.283) while
   large reptiles are the worst (−0.308). A model-free institutional record agrees: of 1,028
   reserves, 17.9% name mammals as a protection target against 1.1% for reptiles.

**Why *Nature Communications*.** The paper speaks to three readerships at once. For anyone who
works with trait data, the methodological result — that compositing dilutes signal and that
naive inference inflates it — applies well beyond vertebrates or China. For macroecologists,
it tests the classical richness hypotheses on trait composition with one named predictor per
hypothesis, across four vertebrate classes on one data foundation. For conservation science
and policy, it audits a megadiverse country's reserve network against Target 3 of the
Kunming–Montreal framework and maps where the functional gap can be closed. We think that
breadth is what *Nature Communications* exists to publish.

**On how the evidence is reported.** We pre-specified refutation conditions for each
hypothesis, and one of them was nearly met: ectotherms respond more strongly to five of six
predictors, not only to thermal ones. We therefore report thermal buffering as **partially
supported**, replace the failed single negative control with a dose–response test across
eight predictors (ρ = 0.74, *P* = 0.037), and carry that qualification into the abstract.
Where earlier analyses failed their own checks, the paper reports the correction rather than
the original result: an apparent amphibian over-dispersion signal disappeared once the null
model weighted species by occupancy (+1.70 to +0.08), and a claim that range maps understate
anthropogenic effects held only for birds once identical cells were compared, and is stated
that narrowly.

**Format.** The manuscript follows the *Nature Communications* Article structure
(Introduction, Results, Discussion, Methods). The main text is 4,288 words, the abstract
198 words and the title 15 words, with 8 display items, 25 Supplementary Figures and 28
Supplementary Tables, all numbered in order of first citation. The use of AI tools in this
work is documented in the Methods.

**Data and code.** All code, documentation, figures and aggregated per-cell results are
public. IUCN Red List and BirdLife range polygons may not be redistributed, so the
species-by-cell matrices are excluded; the scripts rebuild them from the original public
sources with a fixed random seed.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all data sources are
credited. The author declares no competing interests.
