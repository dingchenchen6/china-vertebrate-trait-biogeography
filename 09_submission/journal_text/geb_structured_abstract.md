**Aim** To test whether assemblage trait structure has separable dimensions, which classical
biodiversity hypothesis governs each, whether thermoregulatory mode sets the strength of
environmental filtering, and what this implies for protected-area planning.

**Location** China.

**Time period** Present day (range maps 2022–2024; climate normals 1970–2000; human footprint
2020; reserves 2021).

**Major taxa studied** Terrestrial vertebrates: birds, mammals, reptiles and amphibians.

**Methods** We mapped 2,837 species onto 3,782 50-km cells and defined three functional axes
*a priori* (body size, pace of life, niche breadth) with the same meaning in every class. Each trait and axis was regressed on six named predictors, one per
hypothesis, in spatial autoregressive models tested with the fourth-corner max test; we
contrasted ectotherms with endotherms and evaluated the reserve network against the
Kunming–Montreal target of protecting 30% of each species' range.

**Results** The axes were near-independent (|*r*| ≤ 0.30), and combining traits into a
composite weakened the environmental signal in six of eight comparisons. Climate dominated
trait composition in every class (unique adjusted *R*² 0.24–0.40 by class), and ambient
energy affected ectotherm assemblages about 2.3 times more strongly than endotherm ones
(1.98–2.63 across specifications). Ectotherms also responded more strongly to five of six
predictors, so thermal buffering is only partially supported, although the excess scaled with
a predictor's thermal content (Spearman ρ = 0.74). Naive regression inflated significance
1.27–1.72-fold. Reserves cover 10.36% of the land but bring only 2.9% of species
to the 30%-of-range target, against 50.7% for an optimal layout of the same area; large mammals
were the best-protected trait end and large reptiles the worst.

**Main conclusions** Assemblage trait structure is multidimensional, compositing dilutes it,
and climate filters ectotherm assemblages more tightly than endotherm ones. A network assembled
around species, and historically around flagship mammals, leaves a functional gap whose
direction can be mapped and closed.
