# Cover letter

**To the Editors, *Global Ecology and Biogeography***

---

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, **"Thermoregulatory mode sets the strength of
environmental filtering on vertebrate assemblage traits, and leaves a directional
conservation gap"**, for publication as a Research Article.

**What the paper reports.** Trait-based biogeography has a standard move: collapse many
traits into one composite axis, then ask which environmental gradient explains it. Using
2,837 terrestrial vertebrate species across 3,782 50-km cells of China, we show that both
steps discard information.

1. **Assemblage trait structure is at least three separable dimensions, and compositing
   them costs sensitivity.** Body size, pace of life and niche breadth are near-independent
   (|*r*| ≤ 0.30), and combining traits into a composite axis *weakens* the environmental
   signal in six of eight comparisons.

2. **Climate dominates in every class, but thermoregulatory mode sets how strongly.** Ambient
   energy affects ectotherm assemblages about 2.3 times more strongly than endotherm
   assemblages (1.98–2.63 across specifications; every 95% CI excluding 1). Significance is
   tested with the fourth-corner max test, after quantifying the Type I inflation of naive
   community-weighted-mean regression on these data (1.27–1.72-fold).

3. **The reserve network's functional shortfall has a direction.** China's nature reserves
   cover 10.36% of the land but bring only 2.9% of species to the Kunming–Montreal target of
   30% of range protected, against 50.7% for an optimal layout of the same area. Large mammals
   are the best-protected trait end (+0.283) and large reptiles the worst (−0.308).

**Why *Global Ecology and Biogeography*.** The paper sits at the centre of the journal's
remit. It takes the classical hypotheses that macroecology has tested on species richness —
ambient energy, water availability, productivity, habitat heterogeneity, climatic seasonality — and
tests them on trait composition, one named predictor per hypothesis, across four vertebrate
classes on one data foundation. It also addresses inference problems that *GEB* readers meet
whenever they analyse community-weighted means: the Type I inflation of naive regression,
null models that manufacture clustering in endemic-rich taxa, and composite axes that mean
different things in different clades.

**On how the evidence is reported.** We pre-specified refutation conditions for each
hypothesis, and one was nearly met: ectotherms respond more strongly to five of six
predictors, not only to thermal ones. We therefore report thermal buffering as **partially
supported** and replace the failed single negative control with a dose–response test across
eight predictors (ρ = 0.74, *P* = 0.037). Where earlier analyses failed their own checks, the
paper reports the correction: an apparent amphibian over-dispersion signal disappeared once
the null model weighted species by occupancy (+1.70 to +0.08), and a claim that range maps
understate anthropogenic effects held only for birds once identical cells were compared.

**On length.** We note that the main body (6,477 words) and reference list (100) exceed the
journal's indicative 5,000 words and 50 citations, while the 8 figures fall within the
indicative six to eight. The length comes from four vertebrate classes, three linked
questions, and Methods detailed enough to reproduce every result. If the editors prefer, we
will move parts of the Methods and Results to the Supporting Information.

**Format.** The manuscript uses the journal's structured abstract (297 words), with 25
supporting figures (Appendix S1) and 28 supporting tables (Appendix S2), numbered in order
of first citation. AI use in this work is disclosed in the manuscript, as Wiley policy
requires.

**Data and code.** All code, documentation, figures and aggregated per-cell results are
public. IUCN Red List and BirdLife range polygons may not be redistributed, so the
species-by-cell matrices are excluded; the scripts rebuild them from the original public
sources with a fixed random seed.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all data sources are
credited. The author declares no conflict of interest.
